Subject Guide

Physics

Physics is a method-mark subject. You win marks by showing steps, writing symbols cleanly, and keeping units consistent.

High score rule: Every problem should have: diagram → knowns/unknowns → formula → substitution → answer with units + sig figs.

Assessment (Papers)

Exact paper details can vary by syllabus year. Train the core skills below.

Paper 1

MCQ + quick thinking. Traps are units, sign, and graph reading.

  • Always check units/dimensions.
  • Approximate magnitude (sanity check).
  • Practice timed sets weekly.

Paper 2

Structured problems. Method marks reward clean steps.

  • Write formula before numbers.
  • Show intermediate steps.
  • Draw diagrams for mechanics/circuits.

Paper 3

Data/experiment/option skills.

  • State trend → explain using physics.
  • Evaluate uncertainty + errors.
  • Be comfortable with unfamiliar contexts.

Best Study Method (Physics)

  • 4x/week: problem sets (topic → mixed).
  • Weekly: 1 timed mini-paper to build speed.
  • After marking: redo wrong questions after 24 hours.
  • Build a formula sheet you can derive/explain (not memorize blindly).
Physics mistake pattern: you often lose marks from missing steps, not from not knowing the concept. Fix by always showing your method even if you can “do it in your head”.

Method-Mark Template

  • 1) Diagram: label forces/velocities/directions.
  • 2) Known/Unknown: write symbol values.
  • 3) Formula: correct equation (with reasoning if needed).
  • 4) Substitute: numbers with units.
  • 5) Answer: final value, units, sig figs, direction.
Sanity check: does the value make sense? Is it too big/small? Is the sign/direction correct?

Common Mark Traps (Physics)

Trap 1: Units

Forgetting unit conversions (mm → m, kJ → J).

Fix: convert to SI early and write units every line.

Trap 2: Signs/directions

Negative sign or vector direction mistakes.

Fix: define positive direction on your diagram first.

Trap 3: Missing steps

Skipping method loses easy marks.

Fix: show formula + substitution even if it’s obvious.

Data / Graph Questions

  • Describe: overall trend + anomalies + compare segments.
  • Calculate: gradient/area carefully with units.
  • Explain: connect to physics principle (Newton’s laws, energy, fields, etc.).
  • Uncertainty: mention random vs systematic and improvement ideas.
Always write: slope units and what the slope physically represents.

Quick Practice Checklist

  • Can I solve 8–12 problems without notes and show all steps?
  • Do I convert to SI units automatically?
  • Can I interpret graphs and compute gradients with units?
  • Do I redo wrong questions after 24 hours?
